Abstract

Video diffusion and world models generate plausible videos but do not reliably match the requested camera direction and travel distance. We present DreamTour, a training-free framework that generates camera tours from a single image with more accurate control over camera motion. DreamTour first uses a frozen image inpainting prior to extend the photograph beyond its original field of view while preserving the input pixels. Depth-based rendering of this extended scene along the requested camera path produces approximate target views that serve as spatial guidance. We introduce Layout Steering, which aligns early video latents with these views through decaying low-frequency corrections, leaving later denoising to refine appearance and dynamics. For longer tours, we autoregressively generate the video segments, repeating guidance construction from each segment’s final frame to maintain camera control across the full trajectory. DreamTour improves directional accuracy and travel calibration on VBench while maintaining competitive video quality. On WorldScore, it strengthens camera control and scene consistency across chained movements. Together, these results demonstrate that training-free spatial guidance enables more accurate and consistent camera control in pretrained video diffusion and world models.

Method

Expand the input image once, render the requested path, steer early denoising with decaying low-frequency corrections.

A frozen inpainting prior (FLUX.1 Fill) expands the input image into a canvas in one pass, with the input pixels preserved exactly. A depth estimate, rescaled once to the scene's metric median depth, lifts the canvas into a textured point cloud, and rendering it along the requested trajectory produces a guide video of approximate target views. During the first 40 percent of denoising, Layout Steering adds a decaying low-frequency correction that aligns the video latent with the noised guide latent, and the remaining steps run the frozen sampler unchanged, so the prior supplies parallax, disocclusions and fine detail. For longer tours each window starts from the previous window's final frame, with a fresh canvas and guide. All pretrained weights remain frozen, and this training-free design applies to both image-to-video diffusion models and camera-conditioned world models.

Inside a trained world model: SANA-WM

A camera-conditioned world model follows the direction; Layout Steering calibrates the travel distance.

SANA-WM takes the camera trajectory as an input and still travels only part of the way: on these 1.6 m moves the trajectory alone covers 8–24 % of the request. With its native trajectory input retained, Layout Steering carries the camera the rest of the way (43–65 %) at unchanged direction, and does the same on trajectories the model was never trained on.
